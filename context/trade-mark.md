# Trade mark — what to action at CIPC

Practical brief for the director filing at CIPC. Prepared 22 August 2026.

> **Not legal advice.** This is a working brief so the free steps can be
> actioned immediately and an attorney briefed efficiently. The descriptiveness
> call below is a judgement call and a trade mark attorney may read it
> differently.

## The ask, in one line

Run the free CIPC search **before the logo is locked**, then file the logo mark
in class 36. Everything else follows from those two steps.

## Registering the company protected nothing

`SOLD DIRECT (PTY) LTD`, reg 2026/615735/07, is a **company name** on the
companies register. It stops someone registering an identical *company* name.
It gives no right to stop a competitor trading under "Sold Direct", putting it
on a board, or running ads on it.

A trade mark is a separate application on a separate register — same
institution. CIPC administers both.

## The hard problem: the name describes the service

Section 10(2)(b) of the Trade Marks Act 194 of 1993 bars registration of a mark
that consists exclusively of signs which designate the kind, quality, intended
purpose or other characteristics of the service. "SOLD DIRECT", for a service
that sells property direct, is close to a textbook example.

**Expect an objection to a plain word mark.** That is not fatal, but it decides
the strategy:

- The **logo (device mark)** is the registrable asset. A composite of words plus
  a distinctive device is judged as a whole, so the device carries the
  distinctiveness the words lack.
- Even then, expect the Registrar to require a **disclaimer** under section 15 —
  wording to the effect that registration gives no exclusive right to the words
  SOLD DIRECT apart from the mark. Meaning: we own the logo, not the words.
- The words can become registrable later through **acquired distinctiveness**
  (section 9(2)) on evidence of years of use. A year-3 conversation, not now.

## So the logo choice *is* the trade mark decision

This is why it is worth settling before the designer's options are locked.
Judge them on distinctiveness as well as looks:

| Direction | Protectability |
| --- | --- |
| Arbitrary device with nothing to do with property | **Strongest** — carries the whole mark |
| House, roof, key, door, pin | Weak — every property brand uses them; a common trade symbol adds little to already-descriptive words |
| Typography alone, no device | Weakest — hard to protect, and leaves the descriptive words doing all the work |

## Step 1 — the search. Free, and first

Before any money is spent on brand, signage or a sender number.

Search the CIPC trade mark database (free, on the CIPC site) for:

- `SOLD DIRECT`, and each word on its own
- close variants: `SELL DIRECT`, `DIRECT SOLD`, `SOLDDIRECT`, `PROPERTY DIRECT`,
  `DIRECT PROPERTY`
- in **classes 35, 36 and 42**

Then a plain web search, a domain and social-handle check, and the PPRA register
for a firm already trading under a similar name.

**What you are looking for:** an earlier mark that is identical or confusingly
similar in class 36. If one exists we need to know now — not after the logo, the
signage, the WhatsApp sender and the marketing spend.

## Step 2 — settle the logo

See the table above. The search result may also change which option is viable.

## Step 3 — file

**Applicant must be `SOLD DIRECT (PTY) LTD`, reg 2026/615735/07** — not either
shareholder personally. Investors check that IP sits inside the company.

Form **TM1**. South Africa runs a **single-class system**: no multi-class
applications, so each class is a separate form and a separate fee.

**R590 per mark, per class** (CIPC fee — confirm the current schedule at filing).
Not refunded if the application is refused.

| Class | Covers | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **36** | Real estate services, real estate agency, property listing, insurance and financial services | **First — this is the business** |
| 35 | Advertising and marketing of property, online marketplace, business administration | Second |
| 42 | Software as a service, platform design and development | Third — matters once the platform is licensed outside the company |

**Recommended filing:** device mark in classes 36 and 35 (R1,180), plus the word
mark in class 36 (R590) as a cheap option — examiners vary, it may survive with a
disclaimer, and even a refusal tells us exactly where we stand. **R1,770** in
official fees. Add class 42 when the platform is licensed to anyone outside the
company.

Bare minimum if budget is tight: **device mark, class 36, R590.**

## Step 4 — diary the deadlines

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| 9–12 months after filing | Examination report issued |
| **3 months from that report** | **Response due — miss it and the application lapses** |
| 15–20 months after filing | Acceptance, then publication in the *Patent Journal* |
| 3 months from publication | Opposition window — anyone may oppose (section 21) |
| ~2 years from filing | Registration, on a clean run |

Protection runs from the **filing date**, not the registration date. That is the
whole reason to file early despite the timeline. Registration lasts 10 years and
renews in 10-year periods.

## ™ now, ® only after registration

`™` can be used from today, registered or not. `®` must not appear on the site,
in WhatsApp copy or on signage until the certificate arrives — using it before
registration is a misrepresentation with statutory consequences.

## Do we need an attorney?

A TM1 can be filed without one. Use one here for a specific reason: our mark is
descriptive, an objection is likely, and **the response to that objection is
where the outcome is actually decided**. Filing is the easy part.

Get two or three quotes from SAIIPL member firms — expect several thousand rand
per mark per class on top of the CIPC fee.

If budget is tight: file class 36 in-house now to secure the filing date, and
bring an attorney in when the examination report lands.
